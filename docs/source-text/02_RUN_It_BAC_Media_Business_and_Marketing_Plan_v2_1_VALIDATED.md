RUN It BAC Media will transition from website-first selling to a Revenue Spine business model. # 2. Market Position
## Category
Revenue infrastructure / revenue systems. The company competes against web agencies, automation consultants, AI implementers, conversion consultants, and operational advisors by integrating diagnosis, intervention, implementation, measurement, and optimization.
## Core message
| PRIMARY MARKET MESSAGE / Find where revenue is leaking. Install what is missing. Track what improves. |
## Primary ICP for next 90 days
Established service organizations with enough transaction value that conversion or workflow friction is economically material.
Decision-maker access to founder, CEO, COO, president, managing partner, or executive director.
Fragmented website, CRM, booking, follow-up, reporting, onboarding, or AI usage.
Leadership already believes systems matter and can fund implementation.
Preference for Atlanta-metro access first, then national delivery where the model is proven.
## Secondary ICP
Owner-operated local service businesses remain a valid edition of Revenue Spine when the core problem is website conversion, booking friction, and follow-up. This segment should not define the entire company.

# 8. Sales System
## Qualification
Economic pain exists.
Decision authority is reachable.
Buyer has a meaningful outcome and timeline.
Current state can be evidenced.
RUN It BAC has a standardized intervention or a tightly bounded test.
Buyer can fund the intervention.
## Diagnostic close
Restate buyer objective.
Show verified current-state evidence.
Identify the primary constraint and secondary leaks.
Explain economic exposure as verified data or labeled scenario assumptions.
Present the minimum viable prescription.
Explain why the lower scope is insufficient and why unnecessary scope is excluded.
Present investment and implementation window.
Ask for a decision: proceed, decline, or defer with a date.

# 14. Market-Validated Startup Revision
# Market Reality and Confidence Framework
The 2026 market strengthens the case for Revenue Spine, but it does not justify assuming demand. Small businesses are under cost pressure and increasingly prioritize efficiency, stability, and deeper customer relationships. AI use is widespread, while scaled ROI remains uneven. The operating consequence is clear: RUN It BAC should sell measurable constraint removal, not abstract transformation or AI novelty.
| 2026 Signal | Implication for RUN It BAC |
| Small-business AI use is widespread, but many firms remain early in practical adoption. | Do not sell AI access. Sell one measurable workflow or revenue constraint solved. |
| Cost, unclear ROI, fragmented data, and workforce readiness are major blockers. | Start with bounded interventions, explicit baselines, and human-in-the-loop adoption. |
| Small businesses face high prices and are cautious about expansion and capital spending. | Reduce upfront scope; use fast payback logic and staged expansion after proof. |
| Businesses increasingly want stability and stronger customer relationships. | Emphasize conversion, retention, response, follow-up, and operational reliability. |
| Enterprise AI use is broad, but most organizations are not yet scaling AI deeply into workflows. | Revenue Spine's workflow diagnosis + implementation layer is more defensible than generic AI consulting. |
| CONFIDENCE RULE / No artifact can make a startup 95% certain to produce revenue before the market validates willingness to pay, close rate, delivery margin, and retention. The objective is 95% confidence that the operating system will discover those answers quickly, protect cash, and scale only after evidence. |
## Go-to-Market Sequence
| Days | Primary Job | Offer | Do Not Do |
| 1-30 | Sell and learn | Qualified Revenue Spine Snapshot -> core install | Do not rebuild the app or broaden ICP. |
| 31-60 | Deliver and prove | Core install + measured 30-day review | Do not add new packages. |
| 61-90 | Repeat and delegate | Same install + optimization where ongoing job is real | Do not scale ads until close rate and unit economics are known. |
## Entry Mechanism
Keep the current free 15-minute audit only as a qualification/snapshot conversation. It should not attempt to deliver the full proprietary diagnosis for free. The full diagnostic is either included in a paid install/deposit or sold separately once buyer willingness is demonstrated.

## Pricing Discipline
Do not anchor startup pricing to the old $1,500 package if the economics cannot support founder time and quality.
Do not jump immediately to $25K-$50K merely because the strategy can support higher-value work.
Set a price floor using delivery cost + software/contractor cost + required contribution margin.
Raise price only after evidence shows close-rate resilience, faster time-to-value, stronger proof, or materially larger buyer economics.
Use scope reduction instead of discounting when a buyer cannot support the target price.
